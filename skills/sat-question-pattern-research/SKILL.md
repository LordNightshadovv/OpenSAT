# SAT Question Pattern Research

Use this when a learner or coach asks what public SAT items reveal about a question type, a recent administration, or high-score mistakes. Read `../../docs/public-question-patterns-2026.md` for the source register and current examples, then verify any time-sensitive claim at its original source.

1. Define the scope: official released sample or Question Bank item, dated third-party reconstruction, or first-person recall. Record the source URL, publication or test date, item identifier if available, and whether the key/rationale is official, publisher-supplied, disputed, or unknown. A regional exam date is not one fixed form; do not claim an exhaustive 2026 item inventory from recollections.
2. Work from the exact task operation: claim and evidence, inference strength, local rhetorical function, transition relation, contextual sense, note-writing goal, clause boundary, or modifier referent. Compare the most tempting alternative against the precise constraint it misses. Check the official explanation where available; treat a recalled answer as provisional.
3. Write only a concise topic/operation paraphrase and an abstract trap mechanism in plugin documentation. Link to the item at its source. Do not copy full passages, stems, choices, screenshots, official rationales, or reconstructed question banks into the plugin or learner model. Do not browse signed-in tests, live administrations, or unauthorized materials.
4. Separate three claims: what the source shows, why an option could tempt a fluent reader, and what this learner actually did. A plausible native-speaker trap is a design hypothesis unless there is subgroup performance evidence. A score of 760 or one wrong answer does not identify an item-level cause.
5. Map the mechanism to an existing `diagnostic-taxonomy.md` code only after observing a learner response and reasoning. For practice, specify a wholly original contrastive item and its single-answer validation; keep official/recall examples out of generated-item text. If evidence is weak or the key disputed, leave the case as a research lead rather than a diagnostic rule.

When updating the register, preserve source tiers and dated links. Present an actionable checkpoint that is as narrow as the item requires; do not impose a long checklist on every question or extend the 20-minute Daily Sprint.
