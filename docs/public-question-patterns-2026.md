# Public SAT Reading and Writing question patterns

Updated 2026-09-28. This is a **mechanism index**, not a question bank or a reconstruction of an administration. The first 15 entries point to [College Board's released Digital SAT sample questions and explanations](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/digital-sat-sample-questions.pdf), Reading and Writing questions 1–15. The 2026 entries are separately labeled reports of international test takers or publishers; College Board has not verified their wording, answer keys, item difficulty, or frequency.

The referenced discussion focused on a learner with two reported R&W 760 scores and strong academic reading. That history makes these mechanisms worth **checking**, not diagnosing. No source below supplies native-speaker error rates or this learner's item-level responses. “A fluent reader might choose this” is an analyst's hypothesis, not an observed population result.

## How to read an item

For each question, identify the assessed operation before considering subject matter. Ask what an appealing wrong option **adds, drops, reverses, or fails to do**. A correct-looking fact can fail because it does not answer the task. Use a checkpoint only when its trigger appears, keeping ordinary decisions fast.

| Trigger | Two-second checkpoint | Existing diagnostic hypothesis if observed repeatedly |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Two plausible reading answers | Which word changes scope, certainty, population, or time? | `unsupported_strengthening`, `scope_expansion`, `qualifier_omission` |
| A scientific result sounds persuasive | Does the measured variable actually test the stated claim? | `weak_evidence_threshold`, `qualifier_omission` |
| A sentence “sounds like” contrast | What relation does the **new sentence** add to the preceding claim? | `task_goal_drift`, `structural_misparse` |
| A familiar word seems obvious | What sense is required by this local sentence? | `vocabulary_neighbor_confusion` |
| Punctuation or modifier looks natural | Find the main subject/finite verb or the noun modified. | `structural_misparse` |
| Notes contain many true facts | What writerly goal must the final sentence accomplish? | `task_goal_drift` |
| Very fast, confident, or changed response | What evidence was checked before committing or changing? | `premature_answer_selection`, `unsupported_answer_change`, `overconfidence` |

These codes are **possible mechanisms**. Timing, confidence, and an answer change are observations, not proof of the mechanism. The local learner service retains its recurrence gate.

## Official released sample: 15 inspectable cases

All item IDs below refer to the same [College Board PDF](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/digital-sat-sample-questions.pdf). The descriptions are original summaries; open the PDF for the actual item and official key. The “temptation” column is our analysis of the official distractor logic, not a measured error rate.

| Official item | Assessed operation and topic | Temptation for a fluent reader | Precise check |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| RW Q1 | Main idea; a weaver's dye materials | Promote an interesting detail into a claim about acclaim, rarity, or difficulty. | Choose the relation that covers the whole passage without invented context. |
| RW Q2 | Evidence for a proposed dinosaur sail function | Select a real model difference that does not measure underwater maneuvering. | Match the proposed advantage to the experimental outcome, direction, and comparison arm. |
| RW Q3 | Text evidence for anticipation **and** regret | Choose a vivid quotation that shows only one half of the claim. | Mark both required components before reading choices. |
| RW Q4 | Quantitative evidence; attitudes toward robots in occupations | Cite a true percentage or within-occupation contrast when the claim concerns wide **between-occupation** variation. | Compare the same response category across two occupations. |
| RW Q5 | Inference; prolonged deep sleep | Generalize from one trait to all traits or assert more than the scientists imply. | Bridge the stated risk and prevalence while staying within the subject of sleep. |
| RW Q6 | Word choice; spoken-word poetry in print | Pick a plausible positive adjective that misses the later sound cue. | Substitute the candidate and match the local explanatory phrase. |
| RW Q7 | Contextual meaning of a familiar noun in *The Great Gatsby* | Use a common dictionary sense unrelated to the antecedent in the passage. | Resolve the noun to the trait just described, then choose its local sense. |
| RW Q8 | Word choice; interdisciplinary laboratory | Pick an academic-sounding verb with the wrong direction of effect on disciplinary boundaries. | State whether the boundaries are strengthened, exemplified, or crossed. |
| RW Q9 | Overall purpose; a null result in a posture study | Treat one study as a broad refutation or a critique of other researchers' methods. | Identify why this example was introduced: to limit the strength of a general claim. |
| RW Q10 | Function of an underlined sentence in a character description | Restate the sentence's content or invent a contrast/setting role. | Look at what the **following** sentences develop. |
| RW Q11 | Cross-text response; diversity hypothesis and pool experiment | Declare the theory wholly impossible or save it with an unsupported exception. | Distinguish a plausible prior expectation from what this particular study supports. |
| RW Q12 | Rhetorical synthesis; two sculptures | Select true notes, dates, or a contrast despite a goal of showing similarity. | Write the goal in one verb phrase before comparing options. |
| RW Q13 | Transition; traditional form and unconventional subject | Follow a local word cue and choose addition or result instead of the whole-sentence relation. | Name the concession between the two claims before looking at transitions. |
| RW Q14 | Sentence boundary; dandelion seed explanation | Insert a pause mark inside a long subject–verb span because the sentence sounds breathless. | Find the subject head and its finite verb; do not separate them. |
| RW Q15 | Opening modifier; Maya drama | Choose a human actor as the immediate referent when the opening phrase describes the **work** based on historical events. | Identify exactly what the modifier describes, then place that noun immediately after it. |

The official sample confirms the **design logic** above, not that these items are uniformly hard or disproportionately missed by native speakers. The [College Board Reading and Writing stem guide](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/practice/student-question-bank/reading-writing) and [content-domain framework](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/higher-ed-professionals/sat-validity/content-domains) support the domain labels and operations.

Public post-exam reports are incomplete and drawn from different adaptive forms. The [official scoring explanation](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/scores/what-scores-mean/how-scores-calculated) says both modules contribute, Module 1 routes Module 2, and equal numbers correct can produce different scores. It does **not** publish a fixed easy/medium/hard point-loss table. Consequently, use a few easier items as reliability checks when evidence warrants it, without asserting that an easy miss costs a fixed or universally larger number of points. Pretest items cannot be identified during the test; treat each question as consequential.

## Translation into OpenSAT practice

1. Select one or two candidate mechanisms from **actual** misses, uncertain correct answers, slow responses, or answer changes. Record source provenance and the learner's first/final answer, confidence, and timing when available. A score report without item-level answers cannot identify which mechanism caused a 760.
2. Build wholly original contrastive items with one controlled difference: claim strength, comparison group, sentence relation, modifier referent, contextual sense, or rhetorical goal. Validate one defensible answer and independently inspect every distractor. Never lightly paraphrase the linked items.
3. Mix a small reliability probe with genuinely hard transfer when the learner's evidence calls for it. The purpose is to test attention and rule execution, not to fill a quota or abandon the existing adaptive difficulty recommendation.
4. Compare immediate correctness with delayed/varied transfer and calibration. Treat a one-item success as provisional; promote a recurring error signature only under the existing learner-model evidence rules. Keep the planned Daily Sprint at 20 minutes.
